# Resume writing that survives ATS

Applicant Tracking Systems parse text, not design. Use a single-column layout, standard headings (Experience, Education, Skills, Projects), and a common font. Avoid tables, text boxes, icons, and skill bars.

Lead bullets with a verb and, when the fact exists, a result. Never invent a metric. "Improved query time" is honest; "reduced latency 47%" is only honest if you measured it.

Mirror language from the job description only for skills you actually have. Keyword stuffing with tools you have not used is both an integrity problem and an interview risk.

A master resume stores the full truth. Role-specific versions reorder and emphasize — they do not fabricate titles, employers, or dates.

For students and career switchers, projects and coursework are first-class evidence. Treat a substantial project like a job: problem, your role, stack, and outcome.
